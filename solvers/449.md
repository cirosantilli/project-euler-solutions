# Project Euler 449 Solution - Chocolate Covered Candy

<https://projecteuler.net/problem=449>:

* [449.py](449.py)

This problem asks for the **[volume](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/volume) of a uniform 1 mm chocolate coat** around an [ellipsoid](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/ellipsoid) of revolution:

\[
b^2x^2+b^2y^2+a^2z^2=a^2b^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{x^2}{a^2}+\frac{y^2}{a^2}+\frac{z^2}{b^2}=1
\]

So the semi-axes are **(a, a, b)**: equatorial [radius](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/radius) `a` and polar [radius](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/radius) `b`.

## Key idea 1: Use the parallel-body (Steiner) volume formula

A uniform coat of thickness `t` is the **[outer parallel body](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/outer-parallel-body)** ([Minkowski sum](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/minkowski-sum) with a [radius](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/radius)-`t` [ball](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/ball)).

For any smooth [convex body](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/convex-body) in 3D:

\[
V(t)=V_0 + S\,t + M\,t^2 + \frac{4}{3}\pi t^3
\]

- `S` is the [surface area](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/surface-area).
- `M = \int H\,dA`, where \(H=(\kappa_1+\kappa_2)/2\) is the **average [mean curvature](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/mean-curvature)**.
- \(\frac{4}{3}\pi t^3\) is the [volume](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/volume) contribution of the [rounding](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/rounding) [ball](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/ball).

Therefore the chocolate [volume](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/volume) is simply:

\[
V_{\text{choc}}(t)=V(t)-V_0 = S\,t + M\,t^2 + \frac{4}{3}\pi t^3
\]

For this problem, `t = 1`.

## Key idea 2: Closed forms for a spheroid’s `S` and `M`

Because the [ellipsoid](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/ellipsoid) is a **[surface of revolution](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/surface-of-revolution)** (a [spheroid](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/spheroid)), both `S` and `M` have elementary closed forms.

Let:

- `A` = equatorial [radius](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/radius)
- `C` = polar [radius](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/radius)

### Surface area

- **Oblate** (`A > C`), with \(e=\sqrt{1-\frac{C^2}{A^2}}\):

\[
S = 2\pi A^2\left(1+\frac{1-e^2}{e}\operatorname{artanh}(e)\right)
\]

- **Prolate** (`C > A`), with \(e=\sqrt{1-\frac{A^2}{C^2}}\):

\[
S = 2\pi A^2\left(1+\frac{C}{Ae}\arcsin(e)\right)
\]

### Integrated mean curvature

Using [principal curvatures](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/principal-curvature) of a [surface of revolution](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/surface-of-revolution) and integrating around the generating curve gives:

- **Oblate** (`A > C`):

\[
M = 2\pi C + \frac{2\pi A}{e}\arctan\!\left(\frac{Ae}{C}\right)
\]

- **Prolate** (`C > A`):

\[
M = 2\pi C + \frac{2\pi A^2}{Ce}\operatorname{artanh}(e)
\]

## Key idea 3: Numerical stability near a sphere

At `a ≈ b`, the [eccentricity](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/eccentricity-of-a-conic) `e → 0` and expressions like `atanh(e)/e` and `asin(e)/e` should be handled carefully.

The implementation uses:
- a direct [sphere](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/sphere) special-case, and
- tiny-`e` series expansions for `atanh(e)/e` and `asin(e)/e`.
