# Project Euler 516 Solution - $5$-smooth Totients

<https://projecteuler.net/problem=516>:

* [516.py](516.py)

## Problem Summary

For a given limit $L$, define $S(L)$ as the sum of all [integers](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/integer) $n\le L$ such that [Euler's totient](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/euler-s-totient-function) $\varphi(n)$ is [5-smooth](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/5-smooth-number), meaning that every [prime factor](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/prime-factor) of $\varphi(n)$ belongs to $\{2,3,5\}$. The target value is $S(10^{12}) \bmod 2^{32}$, so a direct scan of all $n\le 10^{12}$ is completely infeasible.

## Mathematical Approach

The key observation is that the condition on $\varphi(n)$ forces a very rigid [prime factorization](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/prime-factorization) for $n$. Once that structure is identified, the sum can be reorganized into a much smaller search over [5-smooth numbers](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/5-smooth-number) and a recursively generated family of admissible [prime](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/prime) products.

### Step 1: Separate the $2,3,5$ Part from the Larger Primes

Write

$$n=2^a3^b5^c\prod_{i=1}^r p_i^{e_i},\qquad p_i>5.$$

[Euler's totient product formula](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/euler-s-totient-product-formula) gives

$$\varphi(n)=\varphi(2^a)\varphi(3^b)\varphi(5^c)\prod_{i=1}^r p_i^{e_i-1}(p_i-1).$$

The factors coming from $2,3,5$ are always [5-smooth](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/5-smooth-number):

$$\varphi(2^a)\in\{1,2,4,8,\dots\},\qquad \varphi(3^b)\in\{1,2,6,18,\dots\},\qquad \varphi(5^c)\in\{1,4,20,100,\dots\}.$$

So every possible obstruction comes from [primes](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/prime) $p_i>5$.

### Step 2: Characterize Which Large Primes Are Allowed

If some [prime](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/prime) $p_i>5$ appears with exponent $e_i\ge 2$, then the factor $p_i^{e_i-1}$ divides $\varphi(n)$. That would force a [prime factor](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/prime-factor) larger than $5$ into $\varphi(n)$, which is impossible. Therefore every [prime](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/prime) $p>5$ may appear in $n$ at most once.

Moreover, when $p>5$ does appear, the factor $p-1$ divides $\varphi(n)$. Hence $p-1$ itself must be [5-smooth](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/5-smooth-number). This proves that every valid number has the shape

$$n=hq,$$

where $h=2^a3^b5^c$ is a [5-smooth number](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/5-smooth-number) and $q$ is a [squarefree](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/squarefree-integer) product of distinct [primes](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/prime) $p>5$ satisfying

$$p-1=2^\alpha 3^\beta 5^\gamma,\qquad \alpha,\beta,\gamma\ge 0.$$

The converse is also true. If $h$ is [5-smooth](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/5-smooth-number) and $q=\prod p$ is a [squarefree](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/squarefree-integer) product of distinct [primes](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/prime) with $p-1$ [5-smooth](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/5-smooth-number), then $h$ and $q$ are [coprime](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/coprime), so

$$\varphi(hq)=\varphi(h)\prod_{p\mid q}(p-1),$$

which is again [5-smooth](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/5-smooth-number). Thus the decomposition is exact.

### Step 3: Turn the Problem into a Sum over Admissible Prime Products

Let

$$\mathcal{H}(x)=\left\{2^a3^b5^c\le x:\ a,b,c\ge 0\right\}$$

be the set of [5-smooth numbers](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/5-smooth-number) up to $x$, and define the [prefix-sum](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/prefix-sum) function

$$A(x)=\sum_{h\in\mathcal{H}(x)} h.$$

Also define the admissible [primes](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/prime)

$$\mathcal{P}(L)=\left\{p\le L:\ p>5,\ p\text{ prime},\ p-1\in\mathcal{H}(L)\right\}.$$

Every valid $n\le L$ is uniquely obtained by choosing a [squarefree](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/squarefree-integer) product $q$ of distinct [primes](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/prime) from $\mathcal{P}(L)$, then choosing a [5-smooth](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/5-smooth-number) multiplier $h\le L/q$. Therefore

$$S(L)=\sum_{q\in\mathcal{Q}(L)} \sum_{\substack{h\in\mathcal{H}(L)\\ h\le L/q}} qh,$$

where $\mathcal{Q}(L)$ is the set of [squarefree](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/squarefree-integer) products $q\le L$ built from distinct [primes](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/prime) in $\mathcal{P}(L)$, including the [empty product](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/empty-product) $q=1$.

Pulling $q$ outside the inner sum gives the main formula

$$\boxed{S(L)=\sum_{q\in\mathcal{Q}(L)} q\,A\left(\left\lfloor\frac{L}{q}\right\rfloor\right)\pmod{2^{32}}.}$$

### Step 4: Why Enumerating $h+1$ Is Enough

The previous step shows that admissible [primes](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/prime) are exactly the [primes](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/prime) of the form

$$p=h+1,\qquad h\in\mathcal{H}(L).$$

So instead of searching through all [primes](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/prime) up to $L$, we only generate [5-smooth numbers](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/5-smooth-number) and test $h+1$ for [primality](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/primality). This is the decisive reduction: the [5-smooth](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/5-smooth-number) list is tiny compared with the interval $[1,L]$.

After sorting the [5-smooth numbers](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/5-smooth-number), the values $A(x)$ can be answered by [binary search](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/binary-search) and [prefix sums](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/prefix-sum). The remaining task is then to enumerate all feasible [subset](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/subset) products $q\in\mathcal{Q}(L)$, which is done naturally by [depth-first search](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/depth-first-search) with the pruning rule $q p\le L$.

### Worked Example: $S(100)=3728$

For $L=100$, the [5-smooth numbers](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/5-smooth-number) are

$$\{1,2,3,4,5,6,8,9,10,12,15,16,18,20,24,25,27,30,32,36,40,45,48,50,54,60,64,72,75,80,81,90,96,100\}.$$

The admissible [primes](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/prime) $p=h+1$ are

$$\{7,11,13,17,19,31,37,41,61,73,97\}.$$

Among their [squarefree](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/squarefree-integer) products, the only ones not exceeding $100$ are

$$1,\ 7,\ 11,\ 13,\ 17,\ 19,\ 31,\ 37,\ 41,\ 61,\ 73,\ 97,\ 77,\ 91.$$

The needed [prefix sums](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/prefix-sum) are

$$A(100)=1258,\quad A(14)=60,\quad A(9)=38,\quad A(7)=21,\quad A(5)=15,\quad A(3)=6,\quad A(2)=3,\quad A(1)=1.$$

So the contributions are

$$\begin{aligned}
  q=1&:&&1\cdot A(100)=1258,\\
  q=7&:&&7\cdot A(14)=420,\\
  q=11&:&&11\cdot A(9)=418,\\
  q=13&:&&13\cdot A(7)=273,\\
  q=17&:&&17\cdot A(5)=255,\\
  q=19&:&&19\cdot A(5)=285,\\
  q=31&:&&31\cdot A(3)=186,\\
  q=37&:&&37\cdot A(2)=111,\\
  q=41&:&&41\cdot A(2)=123,\\
  q=61&:&&61\cdot A(1)=61,\\
  q=73&:&&73\cdot A(1)=73,\\
  q=97&:&&97\cdot A(1)=97,\\
  q=77&:&&77\cdot A(1)=77,\\
  q=91&:&&91\cdot A(1)=91.
  \end{aligned}$$

Adding them gives

$$1258+420+418+273+255+285+186+111+123+61+73+97+77+91=3728,$$

which matches the checkpoint used by the implementation.

## How the Code Works

The C++, Python, and Java implementations all use the same [algorithm](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/algorithm).

First, they generate every number of the form $2^a3^b5^c\le L$ by nested multiplicative loops. The resulting list is sorted, duplicates are removed, and a [prefix-sum](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/prefix-sum) array [modulo](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/modulo-operation) $2^{32}$ is built so that $A(x)$ can be queried quickly.

Next, for each [5-smooth number](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/5-smooth-number) $h$, the implementation tests whether $h+1$ is [prime](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/prime). It uses fast [modular exponentiation](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/modular-exponentiation) and a [Miller-Rabin primality test](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/miller-rabin-primality-test) with fixed small bases, which is sufficient for the numeric range of this problem. Every [prime](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/prime) $h+1>5$ is stored as an admissible [prime](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/prime).

Finally, a [depth-first search](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/depth-first-search) enumerates [subset](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/subset) products $q$ of admissible [primes](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/prime) in increasing order. At each recursive state it adds

$$q\,A\left(\left\lfloor\frac{L}{q}\right\rfloor\right)\pmod{2^{32}}$$

to the answer, then tries to append later admissible [primes](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/prime) as long as the product stays at most $L$. Because the search only moves forward through the sorted [prime](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/prime) list, each [squarefree](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/squarefree-integer) [subset](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/subset) is visited exactly once.

## Complexity Analysis

Let $H=|\mathcal{H}(L)|$, let $P=|\mathcal{P}(L)|$, and let $T=|\mathcal{Q}(L)|$, the number of [subset](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/subset) products actually visited by the [depth-first search](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/depth-first-search). Generating the [5-smooth](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/5-smooth-number) list takes $O(H)$ multiplicative steps, while sorting and deduplicating costs $O(H\log H)$. Building [prefix sums](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/prefix-sum) is linear in $H$.

[Primality](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/primality) is tested only on the $H$ candidates $h+1$, not on every [integer](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/integer) up to $L$. With a fixed-base [Miller-Rabin primality test](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/miller-rabin-primality-test), this contributes roughly $O(H\log L)$ modular-arithmetic work. The [depth-first search](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/depth-first-search) visits each feasible [subset](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/subset) product once and performs one [binary search](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/binary-search) on the [5-smooth](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/5-smooth-number) list per node, so the [summation](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/summation) phase costs $O(T\log H)$.

Thus the [algorithm](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/algorithm) is dominated by the sizes of the [5-smooth](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/5-smooth-number) list and the feasible subset-product tree, both of which are tiny compared with $L$ itself. Memory usage is $O(H+P)$, plus [recursion](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/recursion) depth proportional to the number of [primes](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/prime) currently selected.
