# Project Euler 3 Solution - Largest Prime Factor

<https://projecteuler.net/problem=3>:

* [3.py](3.py)
* [3.lean](3.lean)

## Key idea
To find the [largest prime factor](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/largest-prime-factor) of a [positive integer](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/positive-integer) `n`, repeatedly divide out its [prime factors](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/prime-factor):

1. Remove all factors of 2.
2. Then try odd [divisors](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/divisor) `3, 5, 7, ...` up to `sqrt(n)`.
   - Whenever a [divisor](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/divisor) `d` divides `n`, divide `n` by `d` as many times as possible.
   - Track the largest [divisor](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/divisor) used.

## Why this works
After dividing out smaller [prime factors](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/prime-factor), the remaining `n` shrinks. Once the loop condition `d*d > n` holds, any remaining `n > 1` must itself be [prime](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/prime-number) (and is the [largest prime factor](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/largest-prime-factor)).

## Complexity
Runs in `O(sqrt(n))` [trial divisions](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/trial-division) in the worst case, but is fast here because `n` decreases during factor removal. For `600851475143`, `sqrt(n)` is under 1 million, which runs quickly in PyPy.
