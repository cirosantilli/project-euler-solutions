Under [RSK](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/rsk), the length of the longest non-strictly increasing subsequence of a
word is the first row of the [insertion shape](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/rsk-insertion-shape), and the length of the longest
strictly decreasing subsequence is the first column.  Balanced words therefore
have square [RSK](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/rsk) boundary: first row length equals first column length.  The
alphabet has ten [digits](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/digit), so every relevant shape fits inside a `10 x 10`
square.

For all words over ten [digits](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/digit) with a fixed shape `lambda`, [RSK](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/rsk) gives
`f^lambda * s_lambda(1^10)` words.  The solver evaluates this by the
[hook-length formula](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/hook-length-formula) and [hook-content formula](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/hook-content-formula).

The no-leading-zero condition is handled by reverse-complement [symmetry](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/symmetry).  A
positive decimal string corresponds to a balanced [digit](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/digit) word whose last [digit](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/digit)
is not `9`.  Words ending in `9` are easy to subtract: appending `9` increases
the longest non-strictly increasing length by one and leaves the longest
strictly decreasing length unchanged, apart from the one-letter word `9`.
