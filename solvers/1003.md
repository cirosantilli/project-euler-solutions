The process can be written in terms of [arithmetic carries](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/carry-arithmetic) `q_i = floor(a_i / 2)`,
where `a_i` is the number of stones present when position `i` is processed.
If `b_i` is the singleton indicator, then for `i >= 3`:

```text
2 q_i + b_i = q_{i-1} + q_{i-3}.
```

Having no singletons from position `k` onward is equivalent to
`q_{k-3} = q_{k-2} = q_{k-1}`.  Writing each earlier [carry](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/carry-arithmetic) as this common
tail value plus an offset gives two [linear equations](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/linear-equation) governing the boundary in the [binary digits](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/binary-digit)
`b_i`.  The loneliness condition is just the local rule that selected bit
positions must be at least three apart.

The solver derives the linear [coefficients](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/coefficient), splits the [binary digit](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/binary-digit) positions in two,
and uses [meet-in-the-middle](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/meet-in-the-middle).  The right half is grouped by the boundary sum
and its first two bits; the left half probes matching right states and uses a
sorted tail-offset list to keep only [nonnegative integer](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/nonnegative-integer) tail values.  The two sample
values are asserted before printing `S(80)`.
