# Project Euler 246 Solution - Tangents to an Ellipse

<https://projecteuler.net/problem=246>:

* [246.py](246.py)

This solution turns the [geometry](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/geometry) problem into two **lattice-point counting** problems using only **[integer arithmetic](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/integer-arithmetic)**.

## 1) Converting the “circle + point” definition into a standard ellipse

For a point \(X\), the distance to the [circle](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/circle) (center \(M\), [radius](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/radius) \(r\)) is
\(\lvert r - d(X,M)\rvert\). Because \(G\) lies **inside** the [circle](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/circle), the relevant branch is

\[
d(X,G) = r - d(X,M) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad d(X,G) + d(X,M) = r.
\]

So the curve is the usual [ellipse](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/ellipse) with foci \(M\) and \(G\), and constant sum of distances \(r\).
With the origin shifted to the midpoint of the foci (an **[integer](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/integer)** translation, so [lattice points](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/lattice-point) stay [lattice points](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/lattice-point)), the [ellipse](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/ellipse) becomes

\[
\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1,
\]

with \(a = r/2\) and \(b^2 = a^2 - c^2\) where \(c\) is the focal distance.

## 2) Tangents from an external point and the angle condition

From an external [lattice point](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/lattice-point) \(P=(x_0,y_0)\), the [tangent lines](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/tangent-line) to
\(x^2/a^2 + y^2/b^2 = 1\) have slopes that are the [polynomial roots](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/root-of-a-polynomial) of a [quadratic equation](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/quadratic-equation).
From that [quadratic equation](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/quadratic-equation), the **[discriminant](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/discriminant)** gives a clean expression for the angle between [tangent lines](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/tangent-line).

Let \(u=x_0^2\) and \(v=y_0^2\). Then the acute angle \(\varphi\) between the two [tangent lines](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/tangent-line) satisfies

\[
\lvert\tan\varphi\rvert > 1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
4\,(b^2u + a^2v - a^2b^2) > (u+v-(a^2+b^2))^2.
\]

This inequality depends only on \(x_0^2\) and \(y_0^2\), so the region is symmetric in all quadrants.

## 3) Director circle split (key simplification)

For an axis-aligned [ellipse](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/ellipse), the locus of points from which the two [tangent lines](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/tangent-line) are **perpendicular** is the
**director [circle](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/circle)**:

\[
x^2 + y^2 = a^2 + b^2.
\]

* Inside this [circle](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/circle), the actual geometric angle \(\angle RPS\) (the angle that contains the [ellipse](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/ellipse)) is **obtuse** (> 90°),
  hence automatically > 45°.
* Outside this [circle](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/circle), \(\angle RPS\) is **acute**, and the condition \(\angle RPS > 45°\) is equivalent to
  \(\lvert\tan\varphi\rvert>1\) (the inequality above).

So we count two [disjoint sets](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/disjoint-sets):

1. **[Annulus](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/annulus) count**: points outside the [ellipse](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/ellipse) but *inside* the director [circle](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/circle).
2. **Outer count**: points outside the director [circle](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/circle) satisfying the tangent-angle inequality.

## 4) Efficient counting by scanning \(y\) and solving a quadratic in \(x^2\)

For a fixed [integer](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/integer) \(y\), write \(v=y^2\). The angle inequality becomes a [quadratic equation](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/quadratic-equation) in \(u=x^2\):

\[
u^2 + p(v)\,u + q(v) < 0.
\]

That means all valid \(u\) lie between two real [polynomial roots](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/root-of-a-polynomial). We:

* compute the [discriminant](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/discriminant) and its [integer square root](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/integer-square-root) (`math.isqrt`),
* get a tight [integer](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/integer) bracket for the [polynomial roots](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/root-of-a-polynomial),
* convert the resulting \(u\)-interval into an \(x\)-interval,
* apply the extra constraint \(x^2+y^2 > a^2+b^2\) for the “outer” region,
* and use [symmetry](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/symmetry) to multiply counts for \(\pm x\) and \(\pm y\).

This runs in well under a second in Python.

## Result

The program prints the required number of [lattice points](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/lattice-point) \(P\) with \(\angle RPS > 45°\).
