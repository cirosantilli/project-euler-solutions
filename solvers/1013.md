# Project Euler 1013 Solution - Sum 25 Divisors

<https://projecteuler.net/problem=1013>:

* [1013.py](1013.py)

The divisor-count formula `tau(n) = product(e_i+1)` restricts a number with
exactly 25 divisors to two forms:

- `p^24`, for a prime `p`;
- `p^4*q^4`, for distinct primes `p` and `q`.

Check the first form directly, including its geometric divisor sum.
For the second form, the fourth-root bound on `p*q` makes the search tiny.
The solver calculates this bound exactly with two integer square roots,
using `limit-1` to enforce the strict inequality.

Let `T(p) = 1+p+p^2+p^3+p^4`. Modulo 5, this is zero precisely when
`p = 1 mod 5`; for every other residue it is one. For `p = 1+5*k`, expansion
modulo 25 gives `T(p) = 5 mod 25`. Thus `T(p)` is never divisible by 25,
and `T(p)*T(q)` is divisible by 25 exactly when both primes are 1 modulo 5.

Sieve primes up to the fourth-root bound, retain that residue class, and
enumerate distinct pairs with `p*q` within the bound. Sum `(p*q)^4`, together
with any qualifying prime powers from the first case.

The given first qualifying number is asserted by checking both the bound
just below it and the bound that includes it. No target answer is embedded
in the solver.
