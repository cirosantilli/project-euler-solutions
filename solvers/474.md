# Last Digits of Divisors

## Main Idea
- Write each [divisor](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/divisor) as `2^a * 5^b * y` with `gcd(y,10)=1`.
- Match `2^a` and `5^b` against the required last [digits](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/numerical-digit) (`10^k` modulus).
- Once `a,b` are fixed, the condition becomes an [integer congruence](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/integer-congruence) on `y` [modulo](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/modulo-operation) `2^{k-a} * 5^{k-b}`.
- Count [divisors](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/divisor) `y` of `n!` with residue constraints by [dynamic programming](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/dynamic-programming) over the [unit group](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/unit-group)
  `U(2^u) x U(5^v)`, using the fact that all [primes](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/prime) `!=2,5` are invertible [modulo](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/modulo-operation) that modulus.

## DP Sketch
- For every [prime](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/prime) `p != 2,5`, its [exponent](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/exponent) in `n!` is `E`.
- The contribution of `p` is the [geometric series](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/geometric-series) `1 + p + p^2 + ... + p^E` in the group.
- We maintain [DP](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/dynamic-programming) over [group elements](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/group-element), using the cycle length of `p` in the group to avoid iterating
  `E` times when `E` is large.

## Output
- The program evaluates the two sample assertions and prints
  `F(10^6!, 65432) mod (10^16 + 61)`.
