# Project Euler 100 Solution - Arranged Probability

<https://projecteuler.net/problem=100>:

* [100.py](100.py)
* [100.lean](100.lean)

We need [integers](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/integer) `b` (blue) and `n=b+r` (total) such that drawing two blues without replacement has [probability](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/probability) `1/2`:

\[
\frac{b}{n}\cdot\frac{b-1}{n-1}=\frac12
\;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\;
2b(b-1)=n(n-1).
\]

## Pell equation transformation

Introduce odd-integer substitutions:

- \( u = 2n - 1 \)
- \( v = 2b - 1 \)

Then:

- \( n(n-1)=\frac{u^2-1}{4} \)
- \( 2b(b-1)=\frac{v^2-1}{2} \)

Equating them yields the [negative Pell equation](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/negative-pell-equation):

\[
u^2 - 2v^2 = -1.
\]

Positive solutions \((u,v)\) with at least two total discs produce valid \((n,b)\) via:

\[
n=\frac{u+1}{2},\quad b=\frac{v+1}{2}.
\]

## Generating solutions efficiently

Solutions to \(u^2-2v^2=-1\) can be generated by multiplying by the [fundamental Pell solution](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/fundamental-solution-of-a-pell-equation) of
\(x^2-2y^2=1\), which is \((3,2)\). This gives the [recurrence relation](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/recurrence-relation):

\[
u' = 3u + 4v,\quad v' = 2u + 3v.
\]

The smallest valid arrangement is three blue discs out of four, corresponding to \((u,v)=(7,5)\). Start there and iterate until the total exceeds the supplied bound. The algebraic seed \((1,1)\) represents one disc and cannot support drawing twice without replacement.

The program prints the number of blue discs `b` for the first arrangement with total discs exceeding \(10^{12}\).
