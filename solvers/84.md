# Project Euler 84 Solution - Monopoly Odds

<https://projecteuler.net/problem=84>:

* [84.py](84.py)
* [84.lean](84.lean)

We want the long-run probability of finishing a roll on each Monopoly square, accounting for the two dice, movement cards, Go To Jail, and three consecutive doubles. As in the statement, a player sent to jail leaves on the next roll without waiting for doubles.

### Statistical model

The solver uses a uniform independent card draw whenever Community Chest or Chance is reached. This is a simplifying statistical model of the shuffled decks. The statement literally cycles each deck; conditioning on a particular cyclic order can change the probabilities and even the six-sided modal string. Independent draws are not claimed to be exactly equivalent to averaging every possible cyclic deck order.

This model needs only the square, from 0 through 39, and the consecutive doubles count, from 0 through 2: `40 * 3 = 120` states. It reproduces the statement's six-sided example `102400` and gives `101524` for four-sided dice.

### Exact transitions

For dice with `s` faces, a sum `m` between 2 and `2*s` has `min(m - 1, 2*s + 1 - m)` ordered dice pairs. Exactly one is a double when `m` is even. This constructs the grouped outcomes in linear time in `s`. A third consecutive double sends the player directly to jail without moving or drawing a card. Otherwise, move by the dice sum and apply the square effects.

Use integer card weights with common denominator 256. Community Chest's weights are 224 for staying, 16 for GO, and 16 for jail. Chance's “back three” card at square 36 reaches Community Chest at square 33: its weight 16 splits into 14 for staying there, 1 for GO, and 1 for jail. These account for the second independent card draw without rounding.

Going to jail resets the doubles count; merely landing on square 10 does not. A card that leaves the player on Chance or Community Chest does not trigger another draw.

### Exact stationary probabilities

If `C[i][j]` counts transitions from state `i` to state `j`, every row sums to `D = 256 * dice_sides**2`. Stationary probabilities satisfy

```
sum(C[i][j] * p[i] for i in states) = D * p[j]
sum(p) = 1
```

One stationary equation is redundant. Replace it with the normalization equation and solve the resulting integer linear system using fraction-free Bareiss elimination. Its divisions are exact. Back-substitution returns a common integer multiple of the probabilities, so comparisons and ties require no floating-point arithmetic or convergence cutoff.

The reference explicitly enumerates every dice and card combination and uses ordinary Gaussian elimination, reducing rows by their greatest common divisor. The optimized solver combines equivalent outcomes and avoids repeated rational normalization during elimination. For `n` states, the elimination uses cubic arithmetic-operation count and quadratic matrix storage.

Finally, sum the weights of the three doubles states for each square, sort by decreasing weight and then square number, and concatenate the top three two-digit indices. One-sided dice produce the exact tied cycle `101214`.
