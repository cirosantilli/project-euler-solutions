# Project Euler 34 Solution - Digit Factorials

<https://projecteuler.net/problem=34>:

* [34.py](34.py)
* [34.lean](34.lean)
* [34.py.lean](34.py.lean)

`solve(base=10)` sums the integers with at least two digits in the chosen [numeral base](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/numeral-system) that equal the sum of their digit [factorials](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/factorial). The base may be any integer at least two. Excluding all one-digit numbers gives the original decimal problem at base ten; base two includes the integer 2, whose representation is `10`.

Precompute the factorials from `0!` through `(base - 1)!`. A number with `d` digits has digit-factorial sum at most `d * (base - 1)!`, while its place value is at least `base**(d - 1)`. Find the first width where the place value strictly exceeds that sum. The inequality continues to hold for every greater width, so every positive [fixed point](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/fixed-point) lies below `(d - 1) * (base - 1)!`. Scan from `base` through this derived [upper bound](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/upper-bound), extracting digits by repeated division and using the factorial table.

In decimal, the first failing width is eight. The bound is therefore `7 * 9! = 2,540,160`, and the answer is `40730`.

The Lean proofs establish termination and equivalence to the reference for every base at least two. They prove that the derived bound contains every positive fixed point, and connect both source executions to the sum over that complete set. The search has no fixed digit-width or base cutoff. Its running time grows with the factorial bound, so large bases are mathematically covered but expensive to enumerate.
