Length `k` factors of the [Fibonacci word](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/fibonacci-word) are the [lower mechanical words](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/lower-mechanical-word) with
slope `1 / phi^2`.  If their breakpoints are ordered by
`{m / phi}`, crossing breakpoint `m` changes one adjacent `01` into `10`.
For the decimal value this adds

```text
9 * 10^(k - 1 - m)       for m < k,
1                        for m = k.
```

Thus the required sum can be obtained from the [prefix sums](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/prefix-sum) of those increments
in breakpoint order.

For a Fibonacci [denominator](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/denominator) `q`, that breakpoint order is a modular
progression.  For a general size `n`, choose the next Fibonacci [denominator](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/denominator)
and rewrite the order recursively: the larger of the two previous [Fibonacci
numbers](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/fibonacci-number) gives the base order, and each selected base label contributes one
extra shifted label immediately before or after it.  This reduces the size by
a factor of about `phi` at each step.

The solver carries only four modular summary values for a stream: output
length, total increment, sum of prefixes, and sum of squared prefixes.  A
small piecewise-constant "macro" describes how a base label expands during the
[recursion](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/recursion), so no list of labels or factors is materialized.  The initial [lower
mechanical word](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/lower-mechanical-word) is the prefix of `0` followed by the usual [Fibonacci word](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/fibonacci-word), and
is evaluated with the same Fibonacci block decomposition.  Finally, the generic
geometric increment for `m = k` is corrected to the true final increment `1`.
