# Project Euler 32 Solution - Pandigital Products

<https://projecteuler.net/problem=32>:

* [32.py](32.py)
* [32.lean](32.lean)

We need [products](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/product) `p = a * b` such that concatenating `a`, `b`, and `p` uses each [digit](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/numerical-digit) `1..9` exactly once (no zeros, no repeats). [Products](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/product) must be counted only once even if they have multiple valid [factor pairs](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/factor-pair).

## Key insight: digit-length constraints
The concatenation has 9 [digits](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/numerical-digit) total. The [product](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/product) must be 4 [digits](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/numerical-digit), and the only feasible factor-length splits are:

- `1-digit × 4-digit = 4-digit`
- `2-digit × 3-digit = 4-digit`

Other splits cannot yield exactly 9 [digits](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/numerical-digit) total with a 4-digit [product](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/product).

## Approach
1. Enumerate `(a, b)` over the two valid digit-length cases.
2. Compute `p = a * b` and require `p` to be 4 [digits](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/numerical-digit).
3. Use a [bitmask](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/bit-mask) for [digits](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/numerical-digit) `1..9` to quickly detect:
   - any zero [digit](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/numerical-digit),
   - repeated [digits](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/numerical-digit) within a number,
   - overlaps between `a`, `b`, and `p`.
4. If the combined [bit mask](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/bit-mask) equals all [digits](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/numerical-digit) `1..9`, store `p` in a [set](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/set) to deduplicate.
5. [Sum](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/sum) the unique [products](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/product) in the [set](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/set).

This brute-force over constrained ranges is small (~150k pairs) and runs quickly in PyPy3.
