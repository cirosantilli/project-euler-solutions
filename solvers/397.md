# Project Euler 397 Solution - Triangle on Parabola

<https://projecteuler.net/problem=397>:

* [397.py](397.py)

This repository contains a pure-Python solution (no third‑party libraries) for [Project Euler](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/project-euler) **397**.

## Key ideas

### 1) A chord slope depends only on `x₁ + x₂`
Points lie on the [parabola](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/parabola):

- `P(t) = (t, t²/k)`

For two points `t=u` and `t=v`, the [chord](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/chord) [slope](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/slope) is:

- `(v²/k - u²/k) / (v-u) = (u+v)/k`

So every segment between two lattice x-coordinates has a [slope](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/slope) that depends only on the **sum** of the x-coordinates.

This reduces [angle](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/angle) conditions between segments to equations in the sums:

- At `A`: `s = a+b`, `t = a+c`
- At `B`: `p = a+b`, `q = b+c`
- At `C`: `t = a+c`, `q = b+c`

### 2) 45° becomes a “tangent addition” identity
Using the [dot-product](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/dot-product) condition for a 45° [angle](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/angle) and substituting the [slopes](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/slope) above, the 45° constraints simplify to:

- **Left/right [polygon vertex](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/vertex-of-a-polygon) (A or C):**  
  `k(t-s) = k² + s·t`  (with `s < t`)

- **Middle [polygon vertex](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/vertex-of-a-polygon) (B):**  
  `k(q-p) = -k² - p·q` (with `p < q`)

After dividing by `k²`, these match tangent addition/subtraction formulas:

- `tan(θ+45°) = (tanθ + 1)/(1 - tanθ)`
- `tan(θ-45°) = (tanθ - 1)/(1 + tanθ)`

That’s why the solutions can be parameterized cleanly.

### 3) Reducing to divisors of `2k²`
Both equations can be rearranged to show that certain [linear combinations](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/linear-combination) must divide `2k²`. Concretely, for each `k`:

- Let `D = 2k²`
- Every valid 45° configuration corresponds to choosing a **positive [divisor](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/divisor)** `d | D`

Then the needed sums become simple [integer](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/integer) expressions:

- **[Angle](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/angle) at A (leftmost):**  
  `t = d - k` and `s = k - D/d`

- **[Angle](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/angle) at B (middle):**  
  `q = k + d` and `p = -(k + D/d)`

So for each `k`, we enumerate [divisors](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/divisor) of `2k²` and convert them into `(s,t)` or `(p,q)`.

### 4) Counting triangles becomes counting integer points in intervals
Once the sums are fixed, the remaining free coordinate is just one [integer](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/integer) (`a` for the left [polygon vertex](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/vertex-of-a-polygon), `b` for the middle [polygon vertex](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/vertex-of-a-polygon)). The bounds:

- `-X ≤ a,b,c ≤ X` and `a<b<c`

become a short intersection of [integer intervals](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/integer-interval), so the number of valid choices is:

- `max(0, U-L+1)`

This is **O(1)** per [divisor](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/divisor).

### 5) Inclusion–exclusion for “at least one angle”
If we sum counts for [angles](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/angle) at `A`, `B`, and `C`, [triangles](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/triangle) with **two** 45° [angles](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/angle) (i.e., 45‑45‑90 [triangles](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/triangle)) get double-counted.

We compute:
- `count_A` ([angle](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/angle) at left [polygon vertex](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/vertex-of-a-polygon)), and use [symmetry](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/symmetry) for `count_C`
- `count_B` ([angle](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/angle) at middle [polygon vertex](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/vertex-of-a-polygon))
- `overlap_AB` ([angles](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/angle) at A and B are 45 → C is 90)
- `overlap_AC` ([angles](https://ourbigbook.com/codex/angle) at A and C are 45 → B is 90)

Final formula:

- `F = 2*count_A + count_B - 2*overlap_AB - overlap_AC`
